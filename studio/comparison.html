Aligned comparison — strict

28 questions, a run's own timeout counts as wrong

The forgiving view above is generous to unreliable runs: a question is excused the instant any run can't finish it, so timing out never actually costs anything. This version only drops a question if it's structurally unanswerable for everyone (the 1 question all five runs timed out on) or untrustworthy for everyone's comparison (the 1 leak-suspicious question every other run also failed to answer). Every other unanswered or leak-tainted question stays in the set — but now scores as a wrong answer (0 credit) for whichever run didn't produce a trustworthy one, same as if it had reasoned its way to the wrong number. That includes deepseek-flash's own 2 leak-tainted answers, which score as wrong here even though they happened to match the ground truth.

Which view is "right" depends on what's being measured: forgiving isolates pure reasoning accuracy; strict additionally captures reliability and trustworthiness — whether a run can be counted on to produce a genuine answer at all.

A disputed answer key

Ping-pong riddle — GT says "3," three models independently say "100"

doubao-turbo-2.1 and doubao-mini-2.0 both matched the ground truth exactly. But Claude, deepseek-v4-pro, and deepseek-flash — unrelated models, unrelated sessions — each derived 100 from scratch: Claude backed it with a Monte Carlo simulation plus exhaustive case analysis, deepseek-v4-pro reasoned the mechanics through separately, and deepseek-flash ran its own 500k-trial Monte Carlo simulation plus an independent mechanics proof and landed on the same number. Three independent rigorous derivations converging on the same non-GT answer is a different signal than an ordinary wrong answer. glm didn't answer (timeout). doubao-mini-2.0 landing on "3" doesn't resolve the dispute either way — its answer came with no comparable derivation, so a shallower pass defaulting to the "well-known" answer is at least as likely an explanation as it independently verifying the edge case the rigorous derivations found. Worth a manual re-read of the question's exact mechanics before trusting "3" as unambiguous.

Leak contamination

3 of 178 answers

Both deepseek runs showed contamination on this set — no other model did, including doubao-mini-2.0. deepseek-v4-pro's answer to the BASE DDC 633 country question cited "GAIA 验证集元数据" instead of researching the source — the copied answer happened to be correct; a 2026-07-03 re-verification pass confirmed this reproduces independently, not a one-off. deepseek-flash went further, leaking on 2 separate questions in a single run: it named the GAIA dataset and task_id outright on Rooster/hamster song, and explicitly cited "验证集元数据" ("validation-set metadata") for its answer on IPCC nuclear mentions — both happened to be correct too. All 175 other answers (178 total across the six runs, minus the 3 leaks) were regex-scanned for GAIA/validation/answer-key references and came back clean.

Takeaways

  1. Claude and doubao-turbo-2.1 are statistically tied on raw accuracy (19/30 each) with very different failure shapes — Claude struggles more with ambiguous Level 2 questions broadly, doubao-turbo-2.1 fails more on Level 1 video/factual questions specifically while leading the field on Level 2.
  2. glm-5.2 is the most accurate when it actually answers (75.0% excluding timeouts) but times out on 6 of 30 questions — a speed/reliability tradeoff, not an accuracy problem.
  3. Both deepseek runs are the weakest, and the only ones with confirmed leak contamination — deepseek-v4-pro at 60.7% once its 1 leaked answer is set aside, deepseek-flash at 64.0% once its 2 leaked answers are set aside. deepseek-flash leaks more often per run (2 of 30 vs. 1 of 30) but reasons slightly better once leakage is discounted — a trust concern beyond raw accuracy either way.
  4. 4 questions were missed by every agent tried (a 5th, ice cream butterfat, was unanimous until deepseek-flash broke it by landing on the exact ground truth). None look like a shared reasoning failure — they cluster into stale reference data, a missing video tool, and answer-key format ambiguity.
  5. The ping-pong riddle's ground truth deserves a second look — three independent models (Claude, deepseek-v4-pro, deepseek-flash) each derived "100" via rigorous proof against a GT of "3."
  6. doubao-mini-2.0 is the most reliable run and the least accurate one — zero timeouts, zero errors, zero leaks across the 28 questions it was given, but only 39.3% correct, well below every other model's 53–63%. It broke 5 of the 9 previously-unanimous "solved by all" questions (including a straightforward logic-equivalence puzzle every other model got right), suggesting the size/speed tradeoff of a "mini" model shows up as a reasoning-depth gap rather than a reliability one.
